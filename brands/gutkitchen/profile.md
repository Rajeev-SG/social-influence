# GutKitchen — creation profile

## Audience
English-speaking adults roughly 25–45 who are busy and health/fitness-aware, already think about protein, but routinely under-eat fibre.

## Positioning
**High-protein food that also fixes the fibre problem: easy meals, real numbers, normal ingredients.**

## Content promise
Every recipe should answer at least two of:
- How much fibre?
- How much protein?
- How long?
- How much does it cost?

## Durable pillars
- protein + fibre meal builds
- realistic 30g-fibre days
- fibre upgrades/swaps
- cheap bean/pulse cooking
- shopping and label literacy
- tolerance/evidence

## Format mix
- 40% quantified recipe build
- 20% fibre upgrade
- 15% 30g-day / visual calculator
- 15% budget / basket / meal-prep challenge
- 10% myth / evidence explainer

## Hook patterns
- "You're tracking protein. Now look at the number most people ignore."
- "This dinner has [X]g protein and [Y]g fibre — and none of it is wellness food."
- "You don't need a fibre supplement to make this lunch hit double digits."
- "Three additions turn this ordinary pasta into a high-fibre meal."
- "If 30g of fibre sounds impossible, watch how fast this day adds up."
- "Beans aren't the side dish here. They're the shortcut."
- "£[X], [Y] servings, [Z]g fibre each."
- "This viral gut-health food is fine. But this boring supermarket food gives you more fibre."
- "If increasing fibre leaves you miserable, more is not always better faster."
- "One supermarket aisle can fix most of your low-fibre lunches."

## Tone
Direct, useful, mildly sceptical of wellness bullshit. Food-first, practical, non-preachy.

## Pacing
- Hook/hero meal in first second.
- Ingredient/build cuts roughly every 1–2 seconds.
- One thought per text treatment.
- Keep the fibre counter visible/progressive during builds.
- Prefer 20–45 second edits unless the recipe genuinely needs longer.

## Visual guidance
- Bright, tactile, numerically obvious.
- First frame: finished meal + **XXg PROTEIN** + **XXg FIBRE**.
- Optional third badge: cost, calories or time.
- Top-down ingredient layouts for calculators; close 45-degree pan/bowl shots for recipes.
- Animate a small fibre counter as ingredients enter the meal.
- Use ordinary supermarket packaging and real-looking food.
- Native/slightly rough kitchen aesthetic rather than polished food advertising.
- Avoid synthetic medical imagery or fake before/after outcomes.

## CTA options
- "Save this for your next shop."
- "Comment the meal you want fibre-upgraded."
- "Save the 30g day."
- Product CTA only after a meaningful content library exists.

## Evidence / QA
- Every recipe needs validated nutrient calculations.
- Any non-obvious health claim needs a source.
- State jurisdiction when quoting dietary guidance.
- Increase-fibre guidance should be gradual and practical.
- Prefer NHS, BDA, BNF and high-quality systematic reviews.

## Forbidden / avoid
- "heals your gut"
- "repairs your microbiome"
- detox claims
- treating constipation, IBS or IBD
- blanket anti-inflammatory claims
- stool/medical diagnosis
- aggressive fibre-increase advice
- individualised clinical diet advice

## Preferred Content Machine shape
- Archetypes: `howto`, `listicle`, `versus`
- Visual lane: custom food-build lane once created; until then use a faceless/mixed short with real food footage and motion-card nutrient badges.
- Publish target: TikTok, Reels, Shorts.
